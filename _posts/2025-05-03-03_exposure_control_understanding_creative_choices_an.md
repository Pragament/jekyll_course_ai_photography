# Exposure Control: Understanding Creative Choices and Prioritizing Settings



![As a photographer, you have...](images/image_paragraph_1.png)

As a photographer, you have complete control over the exposure settings of your camera. This can be both empowering and overwhelming, especially when considering the numerous creative options available to you. In this module, we'll explore the concept of reciprocity in exposure settings, examine the importance of prioritizing settings, and provide examples of different scenarios and combinations.

## Reciprocity in Exposure Settings



![When adjusting one exposure setting,...](images/image_paragraph_2.png)

When adjusting one exposure setting, such as aperture, ISO, or shutter speed, you must compensate by adjusting another setting to maintain a consistent exposure level. This is known as reciprocity. For example, if you decrease the aperture (narrower) to increase the depth of field, you may need to increase the shutter speed to balance the exposure.

| Setting Adjustment | Compensating Action | Image |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Decrease Aperture | Increase Shutter Speed | ![Decrease Aperture](images/table_row_1.png) |
| Decrease ISO | Increase Shutter Speed | ![Decrease ISO](images/table_row_2.png) |
| Increase Shutter Speed | Decrease Aperture or ISO | ![Increase Shutter Speed](images/table_row_3.png) |

Understanding reciprocity is crucial for achieving the desired exposure in various shooting scenarios. By recognizing how one setting affects another, you can make informed decisions about which settings to prioritize.
