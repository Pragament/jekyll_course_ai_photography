# Table Data: Camera Categories, Brands, and Key Features

As we delve into the world of cameras, understanding the various categories, brands, and key features is crucial for making informed decisions. In this module, we'll explore the different camera types, their representative models, and the characteristics that make them suitable for specific uses.

One of the most popular and versatile categories is compact cameras. These cameras are designed to be small, lightweight, and easy to use, making them perfect for casual photography, travel, and everyday snapshots. The table below highlights some key features and characteristics of compact cameras:

| Camera Type Category | Specific Camera Type | Brand(s) | Representative Models | Key Features/Characteristics | Typical Uses |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Compact Cameras | Point-and-Shoot | Sony, Canon, Panasonic, Fujifilm, Ricoh, Leica | Sony RX100 series, Canon PowerShot G series (G7 X Mark III, G5 X Mark II), Panasonic Lumix LX series, Fujifilm X100 series (fixed lens), Ricoh GR series (fixed lens), Leica Q series (fixed lens) | Features: Fixed lens (non-interchangeable), compact and lightweight, user-friendly, often with built-in zoom, suitable for casual photography and travel, varying sensor sizes (1-inch, APS-C in some advanced compacts). <br> Range: Entry-level to high-end compacts, price varies with features and sensor size. | Uses: Travel photography, Everyday snapshots, Street photography (discreet), Vlogging (some models), Point-and-shoot simplicity, Backup camera, Situations where small size and ease of use are prioritized. |

As you can see from the table, compact cameras are characterized by their fixed lenses, compact design, and user-friendly interfaces. They're perfect for capturing everyday moments, traveling, or shooting street photography without drawing attention to yourself. Some popular representative models include the Sony RX100 series, Canon PowerShot G series, Panasonic Lumix LX series, Fujifilm X100 series, Ricoh GR series, and Leica Q series.

When choosing a compact camera, consider the sensor size, lens quality, and features like built-in zoom or Wi-Fi connectivity. For example, if you're looking for a more advanced compact camera with better image quality, look for models with APS-C sensors or larger ones. If you prioritize portability and ease of use, consider entry-level compacts with fixed lenses.

In conclusion, understanding the different camera categories, brands, and key features is essential for making informed decisions about your next camera purchase. Compact cameras are a great starting point for casual photographers, travel enthusiasts, and those looking for a backup camera. By considering the sensor size, lens quality, and features, you can find the perfect compact camera that suits your needs and preferences.

As we move forward in this module, we'll explore other camera categories, including mirrorless cameras, DSLRs, and action cameras. Stay tuned for more insights on the world of photography!
