# Table Data: Understanding Camera Settings

**Introduction**

In this module, we will delve into the world of camera settings and explore one of the most crucial aspects of photography: ISO. The ISO setting on your camera controls the light sensitivity of the image sensor, allowing you to adjust the brightness and noise levels in your photographs. In this section, we will examine the different types of ISO settings, their effects on the final image, and common combinations and uses.

**ISO: Understanding Light Sensitivity & Noise**

The ISO setting ranges from 50 to 102400 or higher, with lower numbers indicating less sensitivity and higher numbers indicating more sensitivity. When you set a lower ISO, your camera becomes less sensitive to light, resulting in a darker image if other settings remain unchanged. Conversely, when you set a higher ISO, the camera becomes more sensitive to light, producing a brighter image.

The impact of ISO on noise levels is equally significant. Lower ISOs typically produce less grain or noise in the final image, while higher ISOs can introduce unwanted artifacts, especially at very high settings. Understanding how ISO affects both brightness and noise is essential for capturing high-quality images that meet your creative vision.

**Conclusion**

In this module, we have explored the essential aspects of camera settings and the importance of understanding ISO. By examining the table and its characteristics, you should now have a solid grasp of how to control light sensitivity and noise in your photographs. Remember that low ISOs are suitable for bright daylight conditions, while medium ISOs provide a good balance between brightness and noise. High ISOs, on the other hand, are ideal for low-light situations or when capturing images with a shallow depth of field.

When using high ISOs, keep in mind that image quality can degrade at very high settings. As you continue to explore camera settings, remember to experiment with different ISO combinations to find what works best for your creative vision and the specific lighting conditions you encounter.
